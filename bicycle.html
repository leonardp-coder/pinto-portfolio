Analytical highlights

Patterns worth paying attention to

01

Hypertension & diabetes led

These were the most common health issues reported before participants began regular cycling.

02

BMI improvements varied by gender

Female participants showed a slightly higher rate of BMI improvement than male participants.

03

Asthma cases rose post-cycling

An unexpected increase emerged after cycling—an anomaly to investigate alongside environmental or seasonal context.

04

Enrollment spiked in 2020

New cyclist enrollment remained fairly steady over time, with a notable 2020 surge.

Data preparation

From raw fields to usable signals

Manual cleaning and derived metrics were used to prepare the data for analysis in legacy Excel.

01 / CLEAN

Remove clutter

Drop irrelevant identifiers, survey notes, and unused metadata.

02 / IMPUTE

Handle missing values

Estimate rides/week where possible; use Unknown or mode for categories; remove critical-null rows.

03 / STANDARDIZE

Consistent categories

Normalize gender and health issue labels and unify BMI groups.

04 / DERIVE

Build analysis fields

Weekly ride time, BMI change, engagement tiers, and join year.

Takeaway

A small habit. A measurable signal.

The project highlights positive associations between regular cycling and improvements in BMI and chronic conditions, with differences across age and gender groups. The unusual increase in asthma cases is a reminder to validate trends and consider context before drawing conclusions.
